A small Scottish studio built a top-down game about stealing cars in 1997. What followed was a two-and-a-half-decade rewrite of what an open world could be — and one of the most valuable entertainment franchises ever made.

Grand Theft Auto did not invent crime games, but it invented a template almost every open-world game since has borrowed from: a city that keeps living whether or not you engage with it, and a story told through the freedom to ignore it.

This is the shape of that history — from a bird's-eye car-theft sim made in Dundee to a fictional Florida the whole industry is currently waiting on. Every scene below is an original illustration built for this page, not game footage.

The eras

Six reinventions of the same idea.

1997–99

Top-Down Origins

Built by DMA Design in Dundee, Scotland, the original games were played from directly above — closer to a board game than a movie — and were flagged by parts of the press for rewarding, rather than punishing, lawbreaking.

  • 1997Grand Theft Auto — three fictional cities, missions dialled in from a payphone, and a moral panic that gave the series more publicity than any ad campaign could.
  • 1997GTA: London 1969 — a standalone expansion swapping the setting for Swinging London, right down to left-hand traffic.
  • 1999Grand Theft Auto 2 — added a reputation system tied to rival gangs, in a stylised near-future setting called Anywhere City.
